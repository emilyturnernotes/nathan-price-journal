Asphalt Resurfacing and Prevention: Questions Worth Asking Early

Most driveways do not fail overnight. A hairline crack beside the garage door, a faded patch near the curb, or a puddle that lingers after a rainstorm usually builds up over several seasons. Here are the questions that come up most often, with practical answers.

Does Every Crack Mean I Need Asphalt Resurfacing?

Not necessarily. Cracks tell different stories depending on their shape and spread. A thin line running across the drive, spaced well apart, often reflects age and sun exposure. Those cracks can usually be cleaned out and sealed before they widen.

Pattern cracking is different. When cracks begin to interlock in a web shape, the base underneath may be softening. Consider a driveway on the shaded side of a house where a downspout dumps water in the same spot every storm. The surface may look fine from a distance, yet the edge sinks slightly and new cracks appear along a predictable line. Surface work alone rarely solves that. In a case like this, Asphalt Resurfacing or a deeper repair may be the better recommendation, and an on-site look is the most reliable way to tell.

How Do I Know When to Seal the Surface?

Sealing protects pavement from sun, water, and fuel spills, but it does not fix structural problems. A useful habit is to walk your drive every spring and fall. Look for fading color, dull patches that feel rough underfoot, and loose grains along the edges.

Compare two driveways on the same street. One gets full afternoon sun and is used daily by a work truck. The other sits under a tree and sees mostly passenger cars. The first will likely need attention sooner. That is why a fixed calendar matters less than watching how your own drive wears. When the surface looks worn but still sound, an Asphalt Sealcoat application can restore protection and make future cleaning easier.

Which Everyday Habits Shorten Pavement Life?

Small habits add up. Pivoting a car on one spot scrubs the surface, especially on hot days when asphalt softens slightly. Heavy trailers, moving vans, and loaded trucks stress edges that were never designed for concentrated weight. Oil drips should be wiped up quickly with an absorbent material and a degreaser made for pavement, rather than left to soak in.

Water deserves special attention. Gutters that discharge at the edge of the drive, or a sprinkler that sprays the pavement every morning, gradually weaken the base. Redirecting downspouts and adjusting sprinkler heads costs little and protects your investment. Good Asphalt Driveway Maintenance often depends less on big projects and more on these repeated small decisions.

Where Should I Look for Early Water Damage?

Check the low spots first. After a heavy rain, walk the drive and note where water stands for more than a few hours. Standing water usually means the surface has settled or that drainage is sending water toward the wrong place. Next, examine the seams where the driveway meets the garage apron, the street, or a walkway. These joints are common entry points for moisture.

Keep a simple photo log on your phone. Dating a picture of a crack every few months shows whether it is holding steady or spreading. That record is valuable when you describe the problem to a contractor, because it replaces guesswork with evidence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I wait a season before dealing with a crack?

Narrow, stable cracks often can wait while you monitor them. If a crack widens, collects water, or begins to web across the surface, schedule an evaluation sooner rather than later.

Is sealcoating the same as resurfacing?

No. Sealcoating adds a protective layer to pavement that is still sound. Resurfacing addresses more worn pavement and is usually considered when the surface has deteriorated further. An evaluation can confirm which option fits your drive.

How do I keep my driveway in good shape between professional visits?

Keep water moving away from the pavement, clean oil spills promptly, avoid pivoting on one spot, and inspect the surface each season. These habits form the core of everyday driveway care.

What if I am not sure the problem is serious?

Take dated photos and note how the problem changes over time. Then request a no cost evaluation. You will get a clearer picture without committing to work you do not need.
